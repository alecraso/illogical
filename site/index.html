diff · port · editor · conversation

        
Not just terminals.

        
A terminal is one kind of block. The things you'd switch windows for open beside the pane they're about, in the same tabs and splits, on the same machine, and they come back after a restart. Changes and conversations work as soon as it's installed; dev servers and VS Code are opt-in, because each needs a web origin of its own (how to turn them on).

      

      

        

          
          
What changed

          
Changes opens a diff block for the pane's repository: each file with its +/−, its hunks a tap away. Tap a line for the file, marked there. Both follow the edits as they land, and nothing in them can write, so checking an agent's work from your phone is safe.

        

        

          
          
Your dev server, beside it opt-in

          
npm run dev in a pane, then open its port: the app sits next to the terminal, hot reload and all. Each block is its own origin, so the page can't reach Arugula. When the server dies the block says so, and the page comes back with it. One daemon flag turns it on for the browser on that machine; from your phone, it also needs a domain of yours.

        

        

          
          
VS Code where the pane runs opt-in

          
Open in editor starts VS Code on the pane's machine, at the file and line, with your settings and extensions kept, and it sleeps when nobody has it open. It's shown like a dev server, so it's turned on the same way.

        

        

          
          
Claude Code conversations

          
Any Claude Code session on the machine, from a terminal or the desktop app, opens as a block: prompts, replies, every tool call and its output. Follow one from your phone as it runs, then Continue it right there, with your settings and skills, or Fork it if it's still open somewhere else.

        

      

      
And more: web pages (arugula open example.com), panes from your other machines in this layout, drawn straight from their host, and your own VS Code, Cursor or nvim as tiles in the swarm, with the debugger's stops as cards. Every kind shares the layout, the arugula CLI and the rail of what needs you.

Get Arugula.

      

        

          
macOS

          
apple silicon or intel · .zip

          
Unzip it and drag Arugula to Applications. It isn't notarized yet: the first time, open it, then choose Open Anyway in System Settings › Privacy & Security.

          
Or, in Terminal, curl -fsSL https://illogical.widgets.wtf/install.sh | sh installs the app with no Open Anyway step, along with its service and the CLI, and opens it.

Linux

          
x86_64 · AppImage · .deb

          
The AppImage runs as is (chmod +x it first); the .deb puts it in your apps. Either needs Ubuntu 22.04, Debian 12, Fedora 36 or newer.

windows 10 or 11 · x86_64 · installer

          
Panes run PowerShell, and the app reaches every machine in your account too. It isn't signed yet: when SmartScreen stops it, choose More info, then Run anyway.

It sets itself up

          
service · cli · getting started

          
The first time it opens, the app installs arugulad, the background service that keeps terminals running when windows close, and the arugula CLI. Then Getting started puts it on your phone, in the cloud and in Claude Code: a click each.

Details

        

      

      
On a server, Getting started's steps are commands: tailscale serve --bg --https=443 http://127.0.0.1:7681 for your phone, arugulad join https://control.arugula.io for the cloud, and claude mcp add arugula -- arugula mcp for Claude Code.

      
The quickstart covers the rest, and advanced setup covers throwaway VMs per tab, dev servers beside their terminals, more machines, and iTerm2 as a tmux client.


      

        
Runs on your own machines

        
tailscale · encrypted relay · your hardware

        
Connect through Tailscale or an encrypted relay. Layout and scrollback survive reboots; running processes don’t.

Arugula control · hosted · free beta

      
Every machine, from any device.

      
Arugula control is the hosted service behind Getting started's cloud step. Sign in with GitHub or a passkey, add each machine with arugulad join, and open any of them from your phone, another browser or the desktop app, with or without a tailnet.

      

        

          
What it does

          
accounts · devices · relay

          
Keeps your account, your devices and your machines, introduces them to each other, and relays between them when they can't connect directly. Teams share machines the same way.

        

        

          
What it can't see

          
end to end encrypted

          
What your terminals say. Every connection between a device and a machine is encrypted end to end, relayed ones included, and a new device gets in only when one you already have approves it.

        

        

          
What it costs

          
free during the beta

          
Nothing while Arugula is in beta. It's provided as is, without an uptime promise; if pricing comes, it's announced before it applies. Or run your own: it's one binary in the repository.
