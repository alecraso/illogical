# illogical 0.3.0

The swarm: every pane on every machine you and your team can see, in one
live view, and anyone on the team who may answer can answer an agent and
send it its next instruction.

Install or upgrade: `curl -fsSL https://illogical.widgets.wtf/install.sh | sh`

## New

- **The swarm** (`/#swarm`, *Swarm* beside the tabs). Every pane as a tile
  coloured by kind and lit by activity, clustered by project (or directory,
  outside a repository), machine, kind, session or person. Hover a tile to
  peek at its last lines and click it to open it. On a phone, pinch to zoom
  and tap to open.
- **The "needs you" rail.** Panes that want someone lift out to cards
  bundled by cause ("3 failed on build-02", "2 agents ask"), with actions
  for the whole bundle: allow, deny, answer, dismiss. On a phone the cards
  are a strip along the bottom, and a notification opens at its card.
- **Why a pane wants you.** Every pane that wants you says why (an agent
  asking, a command that failed, a program that exited, a bell, a long
  command done), with a headline and a bundle key.
  `illogical attention --json` lists them; `POST /api/attention/act` acts on
  one pane or a list.
- **What each pane is.** Every pane carries its kind (shell, build, test,
  agent, server, logs, editor, read from the running program), its git
  project, its activity and its title. `illogical ls --json` shows them.
- **Every host at once.** The page keeps a summaries-only connection to
  every machine in its list (yours, your team's, and teammates' that shared
  something with you), so the swarm covers them all. Machines that go away
  grey out and come back; sleeping sandboxes aren't woken to be counted.
  Through illogical control, all relayed machines share one connection.
- **The team's swarm.** A session on a personal machine can be shared with
  a whole team, following its signed roster. Panes know whose they are,
  who's driving and who's looking, so the swarm can cluster by person.
  Private panes stay private, and revoking a share or locking a team takes
  those panes off everyone's screen within a second.
- **Team answers.** Claude Code's permission prompts in an ordinary
  terminal pane become approval cards (through its `PermissionRequest`
  hook, `illogical hook`), as its questions already did. Anyone who may
  edit the session can answer them, from the pane, the rail or a
  notification (on a desktop, from the notification's own buttons). Every
  answer records who gave it: the card ("Allowed by Sam, 14:02"), the pane's
  history, `illogical log --who` and the audit log.
- **Follow-ups.** After an answer, *Send a follow-up* gives the agent its
  next instruction: an agent block's next prompt, or Claude Code's, through
  a background `Stop` hook (`illogical inbox`) that never types into the
  driver's prompt. On someone's own machine it needs their trust first.
- See [*Claude Code in a pane*](../cli.md#claude-code-in-a-pane) for the
  hooks, in one block.

## Changed

- **Clients get deltas.** After the first state, a client gets only the
  fields that changed, batched (attention, questions and drivers within
  40 ms, activity once a second), instead of the whole layout on every
  change. At 500 panes with 50 busy that's 5.3 KB/s per client instead of
  6.6 MB/s, and the daemon's CPU drops from 14.7% to 2.5% of a core. This
  is a protocol change: a third-party client must handle `delta` messages
  (and may `subscribe` to summaries only).
- **Notifications to editors are opt-in.** People other than the owner
  choose which agents notify them (*Notify me about its agents*, or
  `/api/notify`), for the daemon's own push and through illogical control.
  Before, every editor was notified through control.
- **The service worker is built** from `web/src/sw.ts` rather than kept by
  hand, so it can answer a notification over its own end-to-end channel.
- **Floods.** A client behind a flooding pane catches up instead of
  resyncing forever: snapshots are capped and compressed, and after a
  resync the page gets the screen alone (#49). Pages acknowledge what
  they've drawn, and a pane's program waits when the pane can't keep up,
  so keys like Ctrl-C get through during a flood (#52).
- **Snapshots are exact.** What a page draws when it attaches or resyncs
  now matches the terminal: blank rows below the text, the cursor under
  origin mode, and each screen's saved cursor come through (#1).

## Not yet

- Real phones: the phone layouts and notification answers are tested in
  phone-sized browser contexts. iOS may open the card rather than answer
  from the notification's buttons.
- Live preview text in the swarm, keyboard shortcuts, rerun from a failure's
  card, and prompt detection (`[sudo]`, `[y/N]`).
